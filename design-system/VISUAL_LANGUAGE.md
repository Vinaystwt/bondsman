# Visual Language

This document makes the single most consequential creative decision in the
rebuild: which visual territory Bondsman occupies. Everything downstream —
tokens (Task 6), every component (Tasks 7–23), motion (Task 24) — is built on
the territory selected here. It is grounded in two committed documents:
`LOGO_READING.md` (the measured geometry of the approved mark) and
`PRINCIPLES.md` (the six binding rules), and in Part 1 §1–2 (product
definition) and §11/§23 (the split-consequence moment).

Read `PRINCIPLES.md` first. This document does not restate those rules; it
selects a territory that can execute under them.

---

## The three territories explored

Each is a distinct, internally-consistent grammar — a different ground, a
different type direction, a different governing metaphor — not one idea wearing
three accent colours. Each is scored against the Part 2 candidate-quality list:
*accountable, exact, financial, consequential, calm, premium, technical,
human-readable, instrument-like, editorial, architectural, durable,
restrained.*

### Territory A — "Structural Ledger" (working drawing of a settlement machine)

**Grammar.** The product renders as a precise engineering drawing that also
keeps accounts. Cool near-white paper ground, dense charcoal-black ink,
hairline drafting rules instead of card borders, chamfered (cut, never
rounded) corners taken directly from the mark, and a strict split between a
grotesque for prose/UI and a monospace for every machine-checkable value.
Filled containers are built things; outline-only containers are drawings of
things not yet built.

**Described sample.** The policy-result screen is one drawing sheet. Two
adjacent filled panels — *AI interpretation* on the left, *deterministic
policy* on the right — are divided by a single hairline rule that carries the
mantra in small caps. The minimum bond, `2,600`, sits in tabular monospace,
right-aligned, with a slashed zero; the surrounding prose is grotesque. Each
panel corner is cut at the mark's ~41.7° pillar angle. A blueprint adapter in
the margin is the same geometry with no fill — an outline. Nothing glows;
nothing is rounded; the sheet reads as a printed spec.

**Three strongest arguments for.**
1. It is the native habitat of the mark and of Principle 2: the logo is *solid
   ink, filled not stroked* with *zero curves*, and Principle 2 already assigns
   meaning to filled-vs-outline construction. This territory does not decorate
   that rule — it *is* that rule at page scale. Hits *architectural, exact,
   technical, durable, restrained.*
2. The drafting-sheet metaphor makes authority separation (Principle 6) and
   number-vs-prose (Principle 1) look native rather than imposed: annotation,
   attribution, and dimensioned values are how drawings already work. Hits
   *accountable, human-readable, editorial.*
3. Suppressed chroma (ink + one reserved slash signal) leaves the consequential
   accent maximally scarce, so Principle 3 lands with full force. Hits *calm,
   consequential, premium, financial.*

**Three strongest arguments against.**
1. Light paper can read documentary/static — the risk is looking like a
   printed report, not a *live* operational instrument (Part 1 §1). Must be
   answered by live values that visibly advance (Principle 2's activity signal).
2. Chamfered corners are also a cyberpunk-HUD cliché; used carelessly they
   drift toward the anti-slop "generic cyberpunk" register.
3. It demands typographic discipline the previous "marketing shell" never had;
   done lazily (default Inter, boxes-as-cards) it collapses into a generic
   dashboard.

### Territory B — "Settlement Terminal" (dark operational console)

**Grammar.** A near-black monitor ground, monospace-forward, tabular figures
dominant, thin luminous rules, high information density. The register of a
settlement rail / oscilloscope: the machine reporting on itself in real time.

**Described sample.** The action monitor is a dark console. A left state/timeline
column streams attributed entries (`Policy priced the bond`, `Watchdog
submitted a challenge`) in monospace; a right rail holds the parties. Bond
economics render as a live tape. At `ResolvedSlash` the bond figure divides
and the slashed portion takes the one consequential accent against the dark
field.

**Three strongest arguments for.**
1. Most *instrument-like* and *live* of the three; a dark console reads as
   "this is running now," directly serving Part 1's "operational instrument."
2. Monospace-forward density suits *financial, technical, exact* and makes
   Principle 1 almost automatic.
3. A scarce accent burns hardest against black — Principle 3's slash moment is
   visually loudest here.

**Three strongest arguments against.**
1. It sits one careless gradient away from every item on the anti-slop list at
   once — *neon crypto styling, generic cyberpunk, random glow, generic
   dashboard template.* The territory's baseline is the exact slop Bondsman
   must not resemble; it spends its whole budget avoiding its own defaults.
2. Dark high-density is *less human-readable* and *less calm*; it reads tense,
   which fights Principle 5's requirement that trouble states be quiet and
   legible.
3. The mark is *black ink on transparent* — its native ground is light. A dark
   console inverts the logo's own construction and makes the mark a guest, not
   the governing form.

### Territory C — "Bearer Instrument" (printed bond certificate / statement)

**Grammar.** Warm paper, a restrained transitional serif for prose paired with
monospace for figures, wide margins, an embossed/sealed-document feel. The
register of a physical bond note, bank statement, or legal instrument.

**Described sample.** The receipt reads as a printed security: serif heading,
monospace hashes, a sealed footer, generous rag. The Action 27 proof feels like
a document you could file.

**Three strongest arguments for.**
1. *Premium, durable, editorial, human-readable* — the receipt genuinely *is* a
   document ("portable signed receipt anyone can verify," Part 1 §2), and this
   grammar honours that.
2. Warm neutral ground reads as trustworthy and calm; strong for *financial*.
3. Serif prose gives an authority and gravity that pure UI type lacks.

**Three strongest arguments against.**
1. Posture clash with the mark: the logo has *zero curve commands* and reads
   *mechanical, engineered, fabricated* (`LOGO_READING.md`, "Implied posture").
   A humanist/calligraphic serif is the opposite grammar — curves and pen
   modulation — so the wordmark and the mark would disagree on every page.
2. Editorial gravity reads as *content to be read*, not an *instrument to be
   operated* — precisely the "landing page with an app appended" the executive
   verdict condemns (Part 1 §1).
3. Weakest on *instrument-like, technical, architectural*; the document
   metaphor has no natural home for a live-updating monitor.

## Selected territory: A — Structural Ledger

Bondsman renders as a **precise working drawing of a settlement machine that
also keeps accounts.** Cool ink-on-paper, filled-vs-outline construction,
chamfered corners cut at the mark's own angles, hairline drafting rules, and a
hard grotesque/monospace split.

### Core justification (specific to Bondsman)

The mark is *filled, not stroked, with zero curve commands*, and it carries
**two distinct, measured chamfer angles — ~41.7° on the pillars and 62.68° on
the base slabs** (`LOGO_READING.md`, "Stroke weight and terminal treatment").
Territory A is the only territory whose corner treatment can be *derived from
those two numbers instead of invented*: primary structural containers take the
41.7° cut, base/footing elements take the 62.68° cut, and border-radius is zero
everywhere because the mark has no curves. Second, the mark is already built
from **two components each drawn as two separate pieces divided by a gap, at
both top and bottom** — "the split is already the mark's defining structural
feature, present twice, at rest" (`LOGO_READING.md`, "The two splits"). That is
the exact geometry of Bondsman's mechanism: a bond in flight is a single sealed
slab, and at resolution — **split consequence** — it divides along that same
centreline gap into `Slashed to reserve · Challenger reward · Payer refund`
(Part 1 §11, §23; §2 "slashes it when objective evidence proves the action was
wrong"). Territory A makes the page geometry and the mechanism geometry the same
geometry. It is also the only territory that *is* Principle 2 rather than
decorating it: real, settled content is filled (matching the mark), a blueprint
adapter is the identical shape with no fill — a drawing of a thing that cannot
yet execute. A dark terminal (B) would invert the mark's own ink-on-transparent
construction; a serif certificate (C) would contradict the mark's zero-curve
posture. Neither can claim the chamfer angles or the filled/outline logic as
native.

### Concrete attributes (the inputs Task 6 turns into literal token values)

Task 6 owns the exact hex/px/ms values. This section fixes the *direction* those
values must express; a token that violates the direction here is wrong even if
it validates.

**Color temperature — cool-neutral, ink-dominant, near-monochrome.**
- Ground: a cool near-white paper (a faint cool grey, not pure `#fff`), so the
  black mark sits on its native light field. A dark counterpart exists (an
  inverted drafting ground — dark, matte, no glow), but **light is canonical**
  because the mark is ink on transparent; dark mode is the same drawing on
  dark stock, not a different territory.
- Ink: a dense charcoal-black matching the mark's solid fill (not pure `#000`),
  with a small cool-grey neutral ramp for rules, secondary text, and disabled
  states.
- Chroma is rationed to exactly two chromatic roles, both reserved, never
  decorative:
  1. **`consequential`** — one high-chroma, matte (non-glowing) signal that
     appears *only* on the slashed portion at `ResolvedSlash`, nowhere else in
     the system (Principle 3). Direction: a cut-metal / hazard register (warm,
     burns against the cool ground), not a soft "error red." It never touches
     form errors, destructive buttons, warnings, or hover.
  2. **`destructive`** — a *separately named* token for destructive UI controls,
     never equal to `consequential` (Principle 3 requires the separation).
- Everything else — links, focus, "live" state, selection — is expressed with
  **ink weight, rules, and motion of the value, not with a third colour.** Live
  vs Historical is presence-of-activity, not hue (Principle 2). This is why the
  territory needs no generic accent blue.

**Type pairing direction — grotesque + monospace, no serif.**
- **Prose / UI:** a neo-grotesque with closed apertures and mechanical,
  slightly-drawn-annotation character — rectilinear enough to agree with the
  mark's zero-curve construction, *not* a soft geometric round and *not*
  chosen because it is the default on generated sites. Task 6 must justify the
  specific family against the mark, not reach for Inter/Geist by reflex.
- **Numbers / hashes / addresses / basis points:** a technical monospace with
  tabular figures and a slashed zero, carrying every machine-checkable value
  (Principle 1). Prose never borrows this face; this face never carries prose.
- **No serif anywhere.** A humanist serif contradicts the mark's posture; the
  Part 2 spec also explicitly rejects the editorial-serif-plus-hard-accent mix,
  and this territory does not flirt with it.

**Corner treatment — chamfered, derived, never rounded.**
- Border-radius is **0** everywhere; the mark has zero curves.
- Corners are *cut* (chamfered) using the mark's two verified angles: **~41.7°**
  for primary structural containers (panels, the bond block, major surfaces),
  **62.68°** for base/footing elements (resolved-state footers, base bars).
- Chamfers are used *structurally and sparingly* — on containers and the bond
  block, not on every button and input — so they read as the mark's grammar,
  not as HUD decoration.
- The bond block is a single chamfered slab in flight; at resolution it splits
  along the centreline gap (Principle 3), reusing the mark's own two-splits
  geometry.

**Density — instrument-dense, structured, bounded.**
- One dominant information zone plus at most one secondary rail (Principle 4);
  never a grid of ≥3 identical cards as the primary layout.
- Comparable items become **tables or grouped typographic lists**, separated by
  **hairline drafting rules, not boxed cards**. Generous margin around the
  sheet; dense, annotated content within panels — the spec-sheet feel, not the
  dashboard feel.
- Badges are reserved for genuine confusion risk only (Principle 2); no pill
  sprawl.

## Anti-slop declaration

Global Constraints require every anti-slop-list item that is *used* to carry a
written justification, and treat any unjustified use as a defect. **This
territory uses none of them.** The list, item by item:

- Generic purple/blue gradients — **not used.** Chroma is two reserved roles;
  there is no gradient and no generic accent blue.
- Excessive gradients — **not used.** Flat ink fills only, matching the mark.
- Glassmorphism as default — **not used.** Opaque paper and ink.
- Excessive blur — **not used.**
- Random glow — **not used.** The `consequential` signal is matte by
  specification precisely to avoid reading as a glow.
- Neon crypto styling — **not used.** Light paper ground, no neon.
- Generic cyberpunk — **not used.** See the two borderline calls below.
- Huge rounded cards — **not used.** Border-radius is 0; containers are
  chamfered and rule-separated, not rounded card shells.
- Card grids for every section — **not used.** Tables/lists over card grids
  (Principle 4).
- Generic dashboard templates — **not used.** Drawing-sheet layout, one
  dominant zone.
- Generic AI chatbot visuals — **not used.**
- Robot heads / brains / coins / chain links / generic shield motifs — **not
  used.** Iconography (Task 23) derives from the mark's own rectilinear
  geometry, not stock metaphors.
- Excessive pills — **not used.** Badges reserved (Principle 2).
- Gradient text — **not used.**
- Floating decorative blobs — **not used.**
- Random abstract 3D objects — **not used.**
- Fake data visualisation — **not used.** Only real Action 27 values appear
  (Part 1 §29 "no fake price ticker").
- Mixed-source icons — **not used.** Single custom set from the mark geometry.
- Gratuitous motion — **not used.** Motion is minimal and suppressed at the
  edge of trouble (Principle 5).
- Trendy-without-meaning design — **not used.** See borderline call 1.
- Typography chosen only because it's common on AI-generated sites — **not
  used.** Type direction is justified from the mark's zero-curve posture and
  Principle 1, and Task 6 is instructed not to default to Inter/Geist.

**Two borderline calls, pre-empted (both resolve to "not the anti-slop item").**

1. *Chamfered corners could be mistaken for cyberpunk-HUD / trendy-without-
   meaning notches.* They are not: the two angles (~41.7°, 62.68°) are measured
   from this specific mark, applied structurally to encode primary-vs-base
   hierarchy, and used sparingly on containers and the bond block — meaning-
   derived, not trend-derived. A generic product has no reason to cut corners
   at exactly these two angles.
2. *The dark counterpart mode could be mistaken for a generic dark dashboard /
   cyberpunk.* It is a dark drafting ground — matte, no neon, no glow, motion
   suppressed at trouble — and it is secondary; light is canonical because the
   mark is ink on transparent.

## Self-check against PRINCIPLES.md

- **Principle 1 (numbers ≠ prose):** the grotesque/monospace split is a
  first-class attribute of the territory. ✓
- **Principle 2 (construction, not label):** filled = real, outline =
  blueprint is the territory's core mechanic, taken from the mark's solid fill.
  ✓
- **Principle 3 (consequence is a split, not a colour):** the bond block splits
  along the mark's centreline gap; `consequential` is one exclusive reserved
  token; `destructive` is separately named; refund resolves in neutral ink. ✓
- **Principle 4 (bounded density):** tables/lists and hairline rules over card
  grids; one dominant zone plus one rail. ✓
- **Principle 5 (motion stops at trouble):** chroma and motion are both
  rationed; degraded states are static, legible ink frames. ✓
- **Principle 6 (authority named):** the annotation/attribution grammar of a
  drawing sheet is where the mantra and per-actor attribution live natively. ✓

No attribute here reuses the `consequential` value for any non-slash role, and
none merges two authority rows into one undivided block.
